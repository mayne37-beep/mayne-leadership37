Dive Deeper: Understanding the MBTI

What is this assessment?

The Myers-Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI) is based on Carl Jung's theory of psychological types. It maps your cognitive wiring across four dichotomies: where you focus your energy (Extraversion/Introversion), how you process data (Sensing/Intuition), how you make decisions (Thinking/Feeling), and how you organize your life (Judging/Perceiving).

Why it matters

Understanding your type helps you recognize your natural communication style and blind spots. It builds empathy by showing that others aren't being "difficult"—they just process the world fundamentally differently than you do.

⚠️ Remember: It's Just a Preference

Your MBTI type does not put you in a box or measure your competence. Think of it like handedness: you naturally prefer your right or left hand. You can still use your other hand to write, but it requires more conscious effort and energy.

💡 Coaching & Reflection Questions

  • How might your preference for Extraversion or Introversion impact the way quieter team members feel in your meetings?
  • When making important decisions, do you naturally favor logic (T) or values/impact on people (F)? How can you consciously incorporate the opposite perspective?
  • Does your team lean heavily toward one specific trait (e.g., highly "Judging" and structured)? What are you missing by lacking the opposite trait?

Dive Deeper: Understanding the Enneagram

What is this assessment?

The Enneagram is a 9-type personality system that goes beneath surface-level behavior to identify your core fears, core desires, and underlying motivations. It explores the deeply ingrained subconscious "scripts" you use to navigate life.

Why it matters

While other assessments tell you *what* you do, the Enneagram tells you *why* you do it. It is an incredibly powerful tool for developing emotional intelligence, recognizing when you are slipping into stress behaviors, and doing deep "shadow work" as a leader.

⚠️ Remember: It's Just a Preference

You are not a number; you are a human being. The Enneagram is not meant to put you in a box, but rather to show you the box you are already in so you can learn how to step out of it. We all exhibit traits of all 9 types, but we typically have one dominant "home base."

💡 Coaching & Reflection Questions

  • When you are under immense pressure or stress, how does your core fear (e.g., fear of failure, fear of being controlled) unconsciously drive your leadership behavior?
  • How can you communicate your core needs to your team in a healthy, constructive way before you become resentful?
  • What is one "blind spot" your type has, and how can you ask a trusted colleague to help you watch out for it?

Dive Deeper: Understanding Working Genius

What is this assessment?

Developed by Patrick Lencioni, the Six Types of Working Genius model breaks down every project into six distinct stages (WIDGET: Wonder, Invention, Discernment, Galvanizing, Enablement, Tenacity). It identifies which stages give you joy and energy, and which stages burn you out.

Why it matters

It beautifully explains the root cause of workplace burnout. Often, we aren't burned out because we are working too many hours, but because we are spending too much time doing work that drains us. It also highlights why teams get stuck (e.g., having a team full of Inventors but no Tenacity to finish the project).

⚠️ Remember: It's Just a Preference

Just because a stage is your "Working Frustration" does not mean you lack the skill to do it. You are entirely capable of doing it! It simply means doing it for prolonged periods drains your battery rapidly. Conversely, your "Genius" is where you derive natural energy and flow.

💡 Coaching & Reflection Questions

  • Look at your lowest-scoring stages. How much of your current job requires you to live in those "Frustration" zones?
  • Think about a recent project that failed or stalled. Which of the six stages was missing from the team?
  • How can you partner with a colleague whose "Genius" is your "Frustration" to share the load more effectively?

The 6 Types of Working Genius

Wonder (W)

The natural gift of pondering the possibility of greater potential and opportunity in a given situation. People with this genius are comfortable sitting in the ambiguity of "what if?" and observing the environment.

Strength: Naturally sees untapped potential, asks profound questions, and prevents the team from rushing into executing the wrong things.

Limitation: Can get stuck in the clouds; deeply drained by environments that demand immediate tactical action with no time to reflect.

Invention (I)

The natural gift of creating original and novel ideas and solutions. They love a blank whiteboard and are energized by the challenge of solving a problem from scratch.

Strength: Highly creative, breaks through dead ends with out-of-the-box thinking, thrives on generating solutions.

Limitation: Quickly loses interest once the problem is solved; struggles with routine maintenance or rigid procedures.

Discernment (D)

The natural gift of intuitively and instinctively evaluating ideas and situations. They are the editors who can look at a rough idea and instantly see what works and what needs to be changed.

Strength: Excellent at pattern recognition, curating ideas, and providing crucial, accurate feedback to ensure an idea will succeed.

Limitation: Drained when forced to constantly critique without enough context, or when their feedback is repeatedly ignored.

Galvanizing (G)

The natural gift of rallying, inspiring and organizing others to take action. They bring the energy needed to get an idea off the ground and convince people to join the cause.

Strength: High energy, incredibly persuasive, great at getting buy-in, and excellent at initiating the first push of a project.

Limitation: Drained by having to constantly motivate apathetic people or pushing projects through the mundane middle phases.

Enablement (E)

The natural gift of providing encouragement and assistance for an idea or project. They know exactly what support is needed and readily provide it on their own terms.

Strength: Highly responsive, anticipates team needs, deeply supportive, and provides the essential human glue that keeps projects moving.

Limitation: Can feel used or burned out if support is demanded without context; struggles to step in if the vision isn't clear.

Tenacity (T)

The natural gift of pushing projects or tasks to completion to achieve results. They find profound joy in crossing the finish line and ensuring everything meets the standard.

Strength: Highly reliable, overcomes obstacles, ensures quality control, and relentlessly drives tasks to 100% completion.

Limitation: Extremely frustrated by shifting goalposts, abandoned projects, or being forced to brainstorm when there's real work to be executed.

Dive Deeper: Understanding Strengths

What is this assessment?

Based on Gallup's CliftonStrengths, this tool categorizes your natural talents into four broad Domains of Leadership. It is based on decades of research showing that the most successful leaders do not have well-rounded profiles; rather, they are exceptionally spiky. They lean heavily into their specific domains.

Why it matters

Traditional management focuses on fixing weaknesses. The Strengths philosophy argues that fixing your weaknesses will only prevent failure; leaning into your strengths is the only way to achieve true excellence.

⚠️ Remember: It's Just a Preference

A "talent" is just a neutral, naturally recurring pattern of thought or behavior. It only becomes a "Strength" when you actively invest time, practice, and skill-building into it. Furthermore, a strength overused or used in the wrong context can quickly become a blind spot (e.g., executing so fast that you alienate your team).

💡 Coaching & Reflection Questions

  • How might your dominant domain currently be overshadowing other areas of your leadership?
  • Think of a recent success. How did you lean on your primary strength to achieve it without even realizing it?
  • Who on your team possesses strengths in your lowest-scoring domain? How can you empower them to lead in those areas?

The 4 Leadership Domains

Executing

Leaders with dominant Executing themes know how to make things happen. When you need someone to implement a solution and work tirelessly to get it done, you look to this domain.

Strengths: Unstoppable work ethic, deeply reliable, turns abstract ideas into concrete reality.

Watch-Outs: Can struggle to delegate, might bulldoze over team feelings in the name of progress.

Influencing

Leaders with dominant Influencing themes help their team reach a much broader audience. They are comfortable taking charge, speaking up, and making sure the team is heard.

Strengths: Highly persuasive, incredibly visible, great at rallying people to a cause.

Watch-Outs: May over-promise, can sometimes prioritize optics over substance.

Relationship Building

Leaders with dominant Relationship Building themes are the essential glue that holds a team together. They create environments where groups are much greater than the sum of their parts.

Strengths: Unifies teams, deeply loyal, creates high psychological safety.

Watch-Outs: May avoid necessary conflict, can struggle to hold people accountable.

Strategic Thinking

Leaders with dominant Strategic Thinking themes keep the team focused on what could be. They constantly absorb and analyze information to help the team make better decisions.

Strengths: Visionary, highly analytical, excellent at spotting future trends.

Watch-Outs: Can get stuck in "analysis paralysis", often struggles with immediate execution.

Dive Deeper: Understanding DiSC

What is this assessment?

DiSC is a behavioral assessment tool centered on four different personality traits: Dominance, Influence, Steadiness, and Conscientiousness. It plots how active/fast-paced you are versus how thoughtful/moderate you are, as well as whether you are more task-oriented or people-oriented.

Why it matters

DiSC provides a simple, common language for teams to understand how they act, communicate, and handle stress. It is incredibly useful for improving sales conversations, team dynamics, and managerial approaches.

⚠️ Remember: It's Just a Preference

We all possess all four styles to varying degrees. The style you exhibit often changes based on your environment (e.g., your "work self" might be highly Dominant, while your "home self" is highly Steady). There is no "best" style for leadership.

💡 Coaching & Reflection Questions

  • How do you need to adapt your communication style when speaking to a team member whose primary style is the exact opposite of yours?
  • Under stress, do the strengths of your DiSC style become liabilities (e.g., D becomes overly aggressive, C becomes paralyzed by analysis)?
  • What specific working environment brings out the absolute best of your primary behavioral style?

The 4 DiSC Styles

Dominance (D)

Direct, results-oriented, strong-willed, and forceful. You prefer to take charge and focus on achieving goals over maintaining harmony.

Strengths: Decisive, results-driven, embraces challenges, natural leader.

Watch-Outs: Can be overly blunt, impatient, and may overlook details or team feelings.

Influence (i)

Outgoing, enthusiastic, optimistic, and high-spirited. You easily persuade others and thrive in lively, collaborative environments.

Strengths: Persuasive, enthusiastic, team-builder, creative problem solver.

Watch-Outs: Can be disorganized, may over-promise, and sometimes lacks follow-through.

Steadiness (S)

Even-tempered, accommodating, patient, and tactful. You value reliable, calm environments, listen well, and are a deeply supportive team player.

Strengths: Reliable, excellent listener, patient, deeply empathetic team player.

Watch-Outs: Resistant to sudden change, overly accommodating, and avoids necessary conflict.

Conscientiousness (C)

Analytical, reserved, precise, and systematic. You focus on accuracy and quality, prefer to analyze all facts before acting, and value clear rules.

Strengths: Detail-oriented, analytical, objective, maintains high standards.

Watch-Outs: Perfectionistic, overly critical, and can get bogged down in over-analysis.

Dive Deeper: Understanding Kolb Learning Styles

What is this assessment?

David Kolb's Experiential Learning Theory outlines a four-stage cycle of learning: Concrete Experience (Feeling), Reflective Observation (Watching), Abstract Conceptualization (Thinking), and Active Experimentation (Doing). This assessment plots where you naturally prefer to enter that cycle.

Why it matters

Knowing how you learn helps you train faster, ask for the right kind of instructions, and deliver better feedback to your team. If you are an Accommodator managing an Assimilator, throwing them into the deep end to "figure it out" will cause immense frustration.

⚠️ Remember: It's Just a Preference

While you have a preferred "entry point" to learning, effective learning actually requires you to move through *all four stages* of the cycle. You cannot master a skill simply by watching it forever; eventually, you must do it.

💡 Coaching & Reflection Questions

  • When you are forced to learn something in a style opposite to your preference, how do you typically react? How can you manage that frustration?
  • Think about how you usually train or present information to your team. Are you only catering to people who learn the exact same way you do?
  • How can you intentionally push yourself into the learning quadrant you scored lowest in this week?

The 4 Learning Styles

AccommodatingDoing & Feeling

Accommodating learners are "hands-on" and rely on intuition rather than logic. They prefer to take a practical, experiential approach and are attracted to new challenges and experiences.

Strengths: Action-oriented, highly adaptable, natural risk-takers.

Watch-Outs: Impatient, may act on "gut" without enough data, easily bored by theory.

DivergingWatching & Feeling

Diverging learners look at things from different perspectives. They are sensitive, prefer to watch rather than do, and tend to gather information and use imagination to solve problems.

Strengths: Highly imaginative, empathetic, excellent at generating multiple ideas.

Watch-Outs: Can be paralyzed by indecision, struggles to take action, sensitive to critique.

ConvergingDoing & Thinking

Converging learners are problem solvers who use their learning to find solutions to practical issues. They prefer technical tasks and are less concerned with interpersonal aspects.

Strengths: Practical, excellent problem solvers, clear decision-makers.

Watch-Outs: Can be overly rigid, struggles with ambiguity, misses interpersonal cues.

AssimilatingWatching & Thinking

Assimilating learners prefer a concise, logical approach. Ideas and concepts are more important than people. They excel at understanding wide-ranging information and organizing it logically.

Strengths: Logical, systematic, brilliant at synthesizing large amounts of data.

Watch-Outs: Out of touch with practical reality, overly theoretical, struggles with execution.

Dive Deeper: Understanding the TKI Conflict Model

What is this assessment?

The Thomas-Kilmann Conflict Mode Instrument (TKI) measures how you respond to conflict situations along two dimensions: Assertiveness (the degree to which you try to satisfy your own concerns) and Cooperativeness (the degree to which you try to satisfy the other person's concerns).

Why it matters

Conflict is inevitable in leadership. Understanding your default reaction prevents you from going on "autopilot" during tense moments. A mature leader knows how to consciously select the right conflict tool for the right situation.

⚠️ Remember: It's Just a Preference

No single conflict style is inherently "best" or "worst." Avoiding can be toxic if used to dodge responsibility, but it is highly effective if tempers are hot and people need to cool down. Competing can be harsh, but it is entirely necessary during a crisis where rapid decision-making is required.

💡 Coaching & Reflection Questions

  • In what specific situations at work is your default conflict style actively harming the outcome or the relationship?
  • Think of a recent disagreement. What would have happened if you consciously used your *lowest-scoring* conflict style instead of your default?
  • Does your team culture reward one conflict style (like Competing) and implicitly punish another (like Accommodating)? How can you balance this?

The 5 Conflict Modes

CompetingForcing

Pursuing your own concerns at the other person's expense. Best used when quick, decisive action is vital.

Strengths: Quick, decisive, stands up for important principles.

Watch-Outs: Can be domineering, may damage relationships, shuts down other ideas.

CollaboratingProblem Solving

Working to find a solution that fully satisfies everyone's concerns. Best used when both sets of concerns are too important to compromise.

Strengths: High trust, innovative solutions, true win-win outcomes.

Watch-Outs: Extremely time-consuming, can over-complicate trivial decisions.

CompromisingSharing

Finding a mutually acceptable solution that partially satisfies both parties. Best used to achieve temporary settlements to complex issues.

Strengths: Pragmatic, saves time, creates a middle-ground fairness.

Watch-Outs: No one is fully satisfied, can lead to mediocre "watered-down" solutions.

AvoidingWithdrawal

Sidestepping the conflict. Best used when an issue is trivial, you have no power, or when people need to cool down.

Strengths: Reduces immediate tension, prevents unnecessary arguments over trivial things.

Watch-Outs: Issues remain unresolved, can create passive-aggression or resentment.

AccommodatingSmoothing

Neglecting your own concerns to satisfy the other person. Best used when you realize you are wrong or to build social credits.

Strengths: Preserves harmony, builds goodwill, shows reasonableness.

Watch-Outs: Your own needs are ignored, can lead to being taken advantage of or quiet resentment.

Dive Deeper: Understanding Emotional Intelligence

What is this assessment?

Based on Daniel Goleman's framework, Emotional Intelligence (EQ) is broken into four quadrants: recognizing emotions in yourself (Self-Awareness), managing them in yourself (Self-Management), recognizing them in others (Social Awareness), and managing them with others (Relationship Management).

Why it matters

Decades of data show that EQ is the single highest predictor of leadership success, far outweighing IQ or technical skill. You can have brilliant ideas (IQ), but if you cannot read the room or manage your own frustration (EQ), those ideas will never be executed effectively.

⚠️ Remember: It is Highly Developable

Unlike IQ, which remains relatively static throughout your adult life, your Emotional Intelligence is highly malleable. It is a set of learned skills and neural pathways that can be dramatically improved at any age through conscious practice and reflection.

💡 Coaching & Reflection Questions

  • What physical "tells" does your body give you (e.g., tight chest, shallow breathing) right before your Self-Management begins to slip?
  • Look at your lowest scoring quadrant. What is one specific, actionable habit you can practice this week to improve it?
  • How does your current level of Social Awareness impact the psychological safety of the people who report directly to you?

The 4 Quadrants of EQ

Self-Awareness

Recognizing your own emotions and how they affect your behavior. High self-awareness means you know your strengths and limitations.

Strengths: Grounded, understands own triggers, authentic, high self-confidence.

Watch-Outs: Can become overly self-critical or spend too much time focused internally.

Self-Management

Regulating disruptive emotions and impulses. Adapting to change, maintaining positivity, and self-motivating.

Strengths: Calm under pressure, highly adaptable, reliable, pushes through setbacks.

Watch-Outs: May suppress valid emotions or appear detached and unfeeling to others.

Social Awareness

Empathizing with others, picking up on emotional cues, and recognizing power dynamics in a group.

Strengths: Deeply empathetic, reads the room perfectly, compassionate, service-oriented.

Watch-Outs: Can easily absorb others' stress, may become overwhelmed by group politics.

Relationship Management

Developing good relationships, communicating clearly, influencing others, and managing conflict.

Strengths: Excellent communicator, resolves conflict well, inspires and develops teams.

Watch-Outs: Might manipulate situations, or overextend themselves managing others' problems.
