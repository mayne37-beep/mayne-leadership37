Concrete Experience

The Impossible Redesign

Your team has been told that, for one week, it cannot use traditional meetings. You still have to coordinate work, make decisions, solve problems, and keep people informed.

NO MEETINGS
NO EXTRA STAFF
NO MISSED WORK
Do not try to improve the meeting. Redesign how coordination happens without one.

Generate three different ways your team could operate.

Notice what just happened. The constraint forced you away from incremental improvement and toward redesign. You already began exploring, reframing, diverging, connecting, developing, converging, and testing—before seeing the model.

Three Different Questions

Creative thinking has a specific job.

Good problem solving uses several modes of thought. Creativity expands possibilities; critical thinking evaluates them; decision-making commits to action.

Creative Thinking
What could we do?

Generate alternatives, challenge constraints, combine ideas, and find possibilities beyond the first answer.

Critical Thinking
What should we do?

Examine evidence, assumptions, logic, tradeoffs, and the quality of the options in front of you.

Problem Solving
What will we do?

Move from understanding the problem to choosing, implementing, and learning from a course of action.

Creativity is not permission to ignore reality. It is permission to search more widely before reality is used to narrow the field.
How Thinking Becomes Constrained

Efficiency can quietly become a boundary.

Experience helps us recognize patterns quickly—but the same efficiency can make our thinking predictable. The goal is not to reject experience. It is to notice when familiar thinking has become the only thinking.

Mental models

Internal pictures of how the world works can become invisible rules.

Assumptions

Things treated as true even though they may not have been tested.

Functional fixedness

Seeing a tool, process, or role only in its familiar use.

Habit + expertise

Past success can narrow the search to approaches we already know.

Organizational norms

“That is how we do it here” can become a substitute for examination.

Fear of failure

People self-censor before an idea is developed enough to judge fairly.

Premature judgment

Evaluation begins before the group has generated meaningful variety.

Group conformity

Agreement can feel safer than exploring a different frame.

Stage 1

EXPLORE

Investigate before you solve. Curiosity changes the quality of the problem you are trying to fix.

Question Storming

For a moment, solutions are not allowed.

An organization is seeing declining participation in voluntary professional development. Traditional announcements, reminders, and supervisor encouragement have produced little improvement.

Generate five questions before offering a single solution. Use the prompts as inspiration, but write your own questions.

What are we missing?Why is this happening?Who experiences this differently?What has already been tried?What would success look like?What if the opposite were true?
0 of 5 questions written
Strong exploration widens what you know before you narrow what you will do. Curiosity is active investigation—not delay.
Stage 2

REFRAME

The way a problem is stated determines where the mind searches for answers.

Change the Question

Do not solve the first version of the problem automatically.

Original frame: “How do we get employees to attend training?” Create five different frames that change where the mind searches for answers.

MotivationDeliveryWorkflowValueChoiceAccessStakeholder perspectiveReverse framing
0 of 5 reframes written
Reframing is not wordsmithing. A useful reframe changes what counts as a possible solution—for example, shifting from attendance to motivation, from an event to continuous learning, or from organizational delivery to employee pull.

20-Idea Challenge

Push beyond your first answer.

Challenge: How could we improve communication between two organizational units that rarely interact? Generate twenty ideas before evaluating any of them. Short phrases are enough.

Stage 4

CONNECT

New ideas often come from combining existing ideas, borrowing patterns, or changing the source of inspiration.

Analogical Thinking

Choose an outside environment. Then ask what principles from that environment could transfer to professional development.

Choose a lens to reveal a transfer prompt.

Structured Creativity Tools

Structure can stimulate creativity.

SCAMPER changes the search pattern by deliberately prompting different kinds of transformation. Produce at least one idea for every letter.

0 of 7 prompts completed
Now identify the two SCAMPER ideas with the most potential. The point is not that every prompt produces a great idea; each prompt changes the search pattern.
Reverse thinking

Design the opposite or worst result, then reverse the conditions.

Six Thinking Hats

Deliberately shift the mode of attention used to examine an idea.

Attribute listing

Break a product, service, or process into attributes and alter them one at a time.

Morphological analysis

Combine different dimensions or features systematically.

Constraint removal

Temporarily remove a presumed limitation and examine what becomes possible.

Worst Possible Idea

Generate deliberately bad ideas to expose constructive design principles.

OPTIONAL / ALTERNATE PRACTICE LAB

Worst Possible Idea

Design the worst employee onboarding experience you can imagine. Then reverse the failures into useful design principles.

Stage 5

DEVELOP

An idea becomes useful when you can explain how it would actually work.

Idea Development Canvas

Make one idea concrete enough to examine.

Choose one idea from your earlier list and develop it. A vague idea is hard to evaluate; an elaborated idea can be improved, combined, or tested.

Development turns “interesting” into “understandable.” Once an idea is concrete, you can compare it with alternatives and identify what needs to be tested.
Stage 6

CONVERGE

Narrow deliberately. Criteria should shape selection before preference takes over.

Criteria Before Preference

Choose how you will judge ideas before choosing the idea.

Select at least three criteria that matter for this challenge.

Visible criteria make convergence more defensible. Strong teams can also combine useful elements from ideas that do not survive as stand-alone options.
Decision matrix

Score options against explicit weighted criteria.

Impact–effort grid

Compare likely value with the effort required.

Dot voting

Surface preferences quickly—but explain why ideas survived.

Pros / cons

Make tradeoffs visible before commitment.

Must / should / could

Separate essential requirements from desirable features.

Combine ideas

Convergence can create a hybrid rather than crown an unchanged winner.

Idea Tournament

Narrow without shutting down too early.

Carry your 20 ideas into a deliberate convergence sequence. Criteria come before preference, and elimination does not mean useful features must disappear.

Final Concept

Which idea has the strongest potential against your criteria?

Selection is not the end of creative thinking. A promising idea is still a hypothesis. The next question is what you need to learn before scaling it.
Stage 7

TEST

Reduce the cost of learning. Test the idea before defending it.

Design the Small Experiment

What is the smallest test that would teach you something useful?

Do not launch organization-wide. Build a low-risk pilot with clear learning criteria.

A failed experiment is not automatically a failed effort. If the test produces useful information, it reduces uncertainty and improves the next version.
The Core Creative Thinking Model

A repeatable process—not a bag of tricks.

You do not need every tool every time. The value is recognizing what kind of thinking the situation needs next.

Key Takeaways + Transfer

What will you do differently?

Creativity is a discipline.

Creative thinking can be practiced deliberately; it is not reserved for people who identify as “creative.”

Better questions create better options.

The first problem statement is not automatically the most useful one.

Generate before you evaluate.

Premature judgment reduces fluency, flexibility, and originality.

Connections expand the search.

Analogies, combinations, and outside perspectives reveal patterns your normal context hides.

Criteria before preference.

Convergence works best when the standards are visible before a favorite solution takes over.

Testing is learning.

Small experiments reduce uncertainty before large commitments are made.
