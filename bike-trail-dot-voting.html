Worked example

Use this after participants have shared their own ideas.

Frame the group problem

People on the Mount Vernon Trail face recurring conflicts where cyclists, runners, walkers, and skaters share a narrow, crowded path. Repeated tack incidents add an immediate safety hazard.

Consider what each group of trail users needs and what the National Park Service and U.S. Park Police can do.

Combine and number the proposals

Group ideas with the same purpose. For instance, put signs, trail etiquette reminders, and outreach together under proposal 2.

  1. 1

    Respond to immediate hazards

    Make it easy to report hazards and coordinate prompt trail checks and cleanup.

  2. 2

    Clarify how to share the trail

    Pilot clear passing and yielding guidance in busy areas and explain it to trail users.

  3. 3

    Redesign crowded sections

    Study where widening the path or separating uses could reduce conflicts.

Walk through the sample vote

Ask which proposal the group should discuss first. Each voter uses two dots and can put both on one proposal or split them between two.

Proposal for discussion

Use Gradients of Agreement to see how strongly people support the proposal. A question to carry forward is “How would we know whether passing and yielding guidance reduced conflicts?”

What the article reports

Ask before voting

Which part of the problem does each proposal address? How might your vote change if the group’s priority were immediate hazard removal?
