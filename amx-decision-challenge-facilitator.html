The Decision Challenge

Eight rounds. Limited time. One team decision each round. What happens next depends on the choices you make.

Three rules

No cross-team communication
You may not confer with people outside your team unless a facilitator gives permission. This includes verbal, nonverbal, and symbolic communication.
Remain in your assigned area
Do not leave your team area unless directed or given permission by a facilitator.
One team decision
Your team must agree on a single choice for every round.

How the exercise runs

Initial review: 6 minutes to study the scoring information and discuss strategy.

Decision rounds: generally 3 minutes for your team to decide and record one choice.

Representative rounds: when directed, one representative from each team will receive temporary permission to meet.

Do not work ahead. If you have a question about the rules, the facilitator may simply repeat the instructions.

Observer Guide

The observer watches the group experience without participating in the team’s decisions.

Observe the process, not the answer

Observer brief

Place yourself close enough to observe interactions, but do not involve yourself in the activity. Watch who is influential, how decisions are made, how people communicate, and how the group changes over time. You will be asked to provide specific observations during the debrief. If classmates ask what you are doing, you may explain your role.

The intended insight

You can be on the winning team in a losing organization.

The leadership challenge is to recognize when local optimization, internal competition, or unit-level incentives are degrading performance across the larger system.

Maximum organizational cumulative score: 98

Rounds 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 7: AMX. Round 3 is doubled. Rounds 5 and 8 use one of the +28 enterprise patterns: BMY, BMX, or BNX.

This is the deeper lesson: ordinary rounds reward shared moderate gains; squared rounds can require two teams to accept a short-term loss so the enterprise captures a much larger total gain.
